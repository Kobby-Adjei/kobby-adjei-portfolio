Four times I wrote down what “good” means, then held something to it.

A clip may end with narrative curiosity, but its language should feel complete. Suspense feels intentional. Grammatical truncation feels broken. The line from a rubric that decided most of a tournament

That sentence started as “this one just feels off.” Every piece below is the same move: take a judgement nobody can argue with or check, and state it precisely enough that someone else can apply it, disagree with it, or prove it wrong.

A design system, a benchmark, an ablation, and a test gate.

They look like four disciplines. The method is one, applied four times: take the judgement — this feels cheap, this clip is good, this answer is right — and state it precisely enough that a machine or another person can check it.

Two things follow once the standard is written down. Quality holds steady while the implementation changes underneath it, which is how inference cost fell by roughly three quarters without the output getting worse. And a real regression can be told apart from a difference of opinion.
