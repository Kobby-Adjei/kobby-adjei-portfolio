Case two · AI evaluation · FlmLnk

Which model picks the best clip?

FlmLnk turns long videos into short clips worth posting. A frontier model chose which moments to cut, and that call was the most expensive part of the product to run.

The question was whether a cheaper model could do the same job without a drop in quality. Answering it required not trusting my own judgement, so every candidate was scored without knowing which model produced it. The mapping between clips and models was opened only after the last score was saved.

Step one: define what good means

A benchmark needs a written standard, otherwise it measures the preferences of whoever ran it. Before any model was tested, FlmLnk's editor reviewed 64 clips blind and scored them. Reading his comments back, the same point appeared in ten separate notes.

Must end on a statement, not mid statement. One of ten near-identical notes about endings

That became the first rule of the rubric, and it went on to decide most of the tournament. A clip has to end on a finished sentence. Narrative suspense is acceptable, because a cliffhanger reads as deliberate. Grammatical truncation is not, because a cut mid-word reads as a fault in the product.

The rubric, and how the scale was anchoredlayer two

Priorities, in order. First, ending quality, because the payoff or resolved thought has to land. Second, a start the viewer can follow without being dropped mid-sentence. Third, an actual moment such as a reveal, joke, reaction or turn, rather than information that merely happens to be coherent. Fourth, emotional rhythm, which means not cutting on the important word when the reaction is what completes it. Fifth, music needs the hook rather than a random verse. Sixth, duplicates are scored on their own merits, because repetition is a separate concern. Seventh, visual dependence is recorded as metadata and never treated as an automatic penalty.

Scale. Four is publishable as selected. Three is a strong moment one boundary fix away. Two is a useful idea that is materially incomplete. One is no worthwhile moment.

Anchoring. One human score was discarded before the rubric was built. A duplicate clip had been given a zero purely because the reviewer had already seen it twice. Fatigue is not a quality signal, and leaving it in would have taught the rubric to penalise repetition.

Step two: judge blind, then unseal

Every candidate was stripped of its model name, shuffled, and scored one at a time. Knowing that a clip came from a frontier model is worth roughly half a point of unearned credit, and that bias is not something a judge can reliably discount by intending to.

The field largely agreed with itself. Most models found the same moments, including the same confession in the drama reel, the same prayer in the boxing stream, and the same admission of earning nothing in the documentary. The difference between models was where they chose to cut.

The figure worth quoting

Cost per usable clip fell by roughly 76 to 80 percent against the GPT-4o baseline, at a higher blind quality score. It was measured two ways, once per usable clip across all 297 candidates and once per completed case across six frozen videos. The two figures land within three points of each other.

Availability findings, recorded separately from qualitylayer two

A model that refuses or fails is not the same as a model that scored badly, and combining the two produces a misleading leaderboard. Four routes never returned anything because of a data-retention policy mismatch, and those were recorded as unavailable rather than as editorial zeros.

Two models in the OpenAI family refused the same video, a raunchy game show. This is a reproducible content policy boundary rather than bad luck, since the newest of them refused it on four consecutive runs. Any architecture built on those models needs a second model to cover that category.

One newcomer had good editorial judgement and unusable operating behaviour. It took 28 seconds per video, produced one reply that ran for four minutes before breaking mid-sentence, and cost eight times the leader per usable clip. It was eliminated on operations rather than on taste.

Step three: the measurement was wrong

With a leader identified, ten head-to-head pairs went to the editor as rendered video rather than as text. Seven of his ten notes were about endings again. Two things did not add up. He was rejecting clips that had scored as clean, and in several pairs he was rejecting both sides for the same reason.

do not re, both said that instead of return The note that identified the fault

The clip was supposed to end on the line "And do not return." It was ending on "And do not retur". At that point the source audio was pulled and measured directly.

  • The transcript placed the end of the line at 237.44 seconds. Every model cut there, which was the correct decision given the information available to them.
  • The word actually finishes at 237.86 seconds. The speech-to-text boundary was 0.42 seconds early and fell inside the word.
  • There is a 0.10 second dip in the middle of the word. The audio finisher used a silence detector, which treated that dip as the end of speech and cut on the exact gap that caused the problem.
  • The previous fix padded every cut by 0.12 seconds. It needed 0.42, so it could not have worked.

Four of the ten comparisons had therefore been decided by a rendering fault rather than by editorial judgement. One of those four was the comparison testing whether a cheap model matched an expensive one, and it returned "neither" because both clips were truncated identically. The models were correct, the transcript was wrong, and the renderer trusted the transcript.

Music failed for a different reason. The word "facts" was rendering as "fa". A trailing "cts" is high frequency and low energy, which makes it invisible under a bass-heavy master, and that track never goes quiet, so any rule that searches forward for a pause runs to its limit without finding one.

The corrected finisher, and how it was verifiedlayer two

The rule. Starting from the model's chosen end, walk forward over sound, counting both broadband energy and high frequency sibilance. Skip any gap shorter than 0.15 seconds, since those fall inside words. Stop at the first real pause, add a 0.08 second tail, and cap the extension at 0.60 seconds. Allow 0.30 seconds of slack past the next transcript segment, because the transcript boundary itself carries around 0.4 seconds of error. Continuous music is detected by a dynamic range under 20 dB, and switches the rule to tracking the vocal alone.

Verification. On the drama clip, high frequency energy in the final 100 milliseconds fell from minus 14.8 dB to minus 31.5 dB, meaning the word now completes and the audio settles before the clip ends. On the music clip, the sibilance ratio fell from minus 9.0 dB, where the consonant was still sounding at the cut, to minus 19.7 dB.

The part that matters most. On four of the twenty clips the tool refuses to act. In those cases the audio runs straight from the chosen end into the next thought with no pause available, so no trim can complete the sentence, and padding would only attach part of the next line. Those four are labelled as model selection failures and left untouched. A tool that hides a model's mistakes is worse than no tool.

Current state

The re-render is built and waiting on a human pass. It uses the same models and the same selected moments with corrected cuts, and the pair order and sides are reshuffled so the previous session cannot influence the result. It separates model quality from render quality, and it costs nothing to run because no model is called again.

No winner has been declared. The human result is currently unstable and confounded by the rendering fault. The candidate repair pass built for this round went one win, one loss and two ties against unrepaired clips while consuming 37 percent of the run budget, so on current evidence it has not earned a place in production.

Open questions

Ten pairs is directional rather than statistical. The question of whether a cheap model matches an expensive one is still open, because the comparison testing it was invalidated by the render. A music case with no detectable pause remains the weakest part of the system.

The general lesson is that most of the work in an evaluation goes into confirming that the measurement measures what it claims to. For a week the numbers indicated that the models were failing at endings. Half of that signal was a tenth of a second of audio.
