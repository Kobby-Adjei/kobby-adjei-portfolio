Writing down what “good” means, then holding a product to it.

Two pieces of work at FlmLnk, a marketing tool for filmmakers. The first turned a product's design decisions into stated principles and a log of every change. The second turned an editor's judgement into a rubric, then scored 23 AI models against it blind.

Both answer the same question. What makes this good, and how would anyone know if it got worse?

Why these two sit together

A design system and a model benchmark look like separate disciplines. The method is the same one applied twice.

In both cases the difficult part is not the building. It is taking a judgement like "this feels cheap" or "this clip is good" and stating it precisely enough that another person can apply it, disagree with it, or show that it is wrong. Principles do that for a design. A rubric and a blind protocol do it for a model.

Two things follow once the standard is written down. Quality can be held steady while the implementation changes underneath it, which is how the model work reduced inference cost by roughly three quarters without the output getting worse. A genuine regression can also be told apart from a difference of opinion.

A clip may end with narrative curiosity, but its language should feel complete. Suspense feels intentional. Grammatical truncation feels broken. The line from the rubric that decided most of the tournament
