Where Multimodal Models Lose Audio Information

Project K is my research project. I test AI models that watch video clips and listen to the sound in them. Sometimes a model hears a sound, but its answer leaves the sound out. I find out where the sound got lost. First I test from outside, by asking questions. Then I test four models that anyone can open and check. Last, I go inside one model and look at how it works, step by step.

The question

More and more, these models write reports that people act on. Think of a home robot that watches the stove. Or a camera in the home of an older person who lives alone. Or a safety monitor that has to hear an alarm. If the report leaves out a sound, the person reading it acts on the wrong picture of the world.

So I do not just ask if a model fails. I ask where it fails. If we know the exact place the sound gets lost, that is a place someone can fix.

Two rules for every test. I use the same files and the same questions, and I change only one thing at a time. And I only claim what the test really showed, no more.

First tests: asking from outside

The first model is called Pegasus 1.6. I cannot open it. I can only send it clips and read its answers. So I asked in five ways.

I sent the same clip with the sound on and with the sound off. The answers came back the same, word for word. The sound made no change at all.

I took one still picture and put seven different soundtracks on it, all at the same loudness. The model reported the spoken words every time. It never reported the beeps, the running water, the vacuum cleaner, or the frying sound. Not once.

I put spoken words on top of a vacuum cleaner sound. The vacuum sound changed the report. The words "the blue kettle" came back as "Canon". But the answer still said there was no other sound. So the vacuum was strong enough to change the words, but the model never said it was there.

Here is the claim, only as big as the tests allow. On these clips, through this service, the model only reports what the spoken words carry. Other sounds do not reach its answers. I cannot see inside this model, so I do not claim it cannot hear. I only claim what its answers show.

Going inside one model

The last model, Qwen2.5-Omni-3B, is open. So I could stop asking it questions and start looking inside. I used two tools. Small checker programs read the model's inner steps and tell me what information is still there. And I did a kind of transplant: I took the inner state from one clip and pasted it into the run of a second clip, then watched how the answer changed. Everything in this part is about this one model only.

The sound is still there at every step. The checkers found the sound, its pitch, and its timing at every one of the model's inner steps, from the first to the last. Inside, this model does not lose the sound at all.

But its yes is set in the wrong place. I asked the model if a tone was high or low. It said yes to almost every tone, high and low. It got about half wrong. That looks like it cannot tell high from low. But when a model answers yes or no, it first makes a score, then turns the score into a word. Its scores were right. High tones got high scores. Low tones got low scores. The problem was the cut-off point where the score turns into the word yes. The cut-off was in the wrong place. When I moved the cut-off to where its own scores pointed, all 360 test tones came out right.

A transplant changes the answer. I pasted the inner state of a clip with one tone into a run of a clip with a different tone. The answer moved to the first clip's tone. This worked fully up to about step 20, worked about half at step 22, and did not work at all by step 24. The pasted state even carried the exact pitch of the first tone, more detail than the answer itself used.

The answer reads the parts that come after the sound. In the later steps, I held the parts that come after the sound fixed to the second clip's own state, while the transplant ran. The transplant's effect almost fully went away. So to reach the answer, what the sound holds has to be copied into the parts that come after it.

Even the name moves with the transplant. When the pasted clip had a high tone, the second clip answered with the word "beep". That happened 24 times out of 24. Then I pasted a clip that matched on both pitch and timing, so the paste changed neither fact. The name never moved. 0 times out of 24. So the name moves because the pitch crossed, not just because I pasted something.

Where it stands

The inside tests on this one model are done, and the results are fixed in place. I wrote down each result, how strong it is, and what it does not prove. I also wrote the tests down as a set of steps, so the same tests can run on any model later. All the result files and test code are public in the Project K repository. Two reviewers checked the work again and again. They found real mistakes. I fixed them, and the fixes are written in the record with dates. The outside tests, with the exact questions and files, are in the reproducibility gist, so anyone can run them again.

What is left is written down too. The same inside tests still have to run on MiniCPM, under the same steps, when I take them up. And the bigger study comes next: sounds in the home, and the machines that will report on them. The yes and no test for Pegasus is finished too: all 60 of its answers are in. I also ran the main questions again at a warmer setting, temperature 0.7, and the answers held.

The hearing was fine. Inside the one model I could open, the sound was there at every step. The problem was in the answer, and I found the place where it happens.
